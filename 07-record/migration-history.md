## 毗陵吕氏族谱 记录

* 1653年版原序

```text
吾呂氏之由來遠矣自唐及宋有稱河東長興侍郎院派
者有稱幽州天福侍郎院派者有稱汲郡顯德侍郎院派
者大抵皆在大江以北其徙居於吳越則自靖康南渡時
舜徒公始遷婺州後更散居他郡
```

## 现代汉语 标点

毗陵吕氏完整迁徙源流

结合1653年版《毗陵吕氏族谱》原始记载与可考证的吕氏远祖世系脉络，这支吕氏的数百年迁徙路径，是中古士族由北向南逐步播迁的典型缩影，完整历程可分为五个核心阶段：

第一阶段：魏晋至盛唐 河东-洛阳吕氏的根基确立

毗陵吕氏的远源可追溯至盛唐河东吕氏一脉，始祖为唐开元二十二年进士吕延之，他官至御史中丞、浙东道节度使，家族世居河东地区，是唐代吕氏最核心的名门支派之一。

吕延之之子吕渭官拜礼部侍郎、太子右庶子，延续了家族的官宦文脉；吕渭之子吕温更是中唐著名文学家，官至衡州刺史，为河东吕氏奠定了深厚的声望基础。

这支家族后续逐步迁居洛阳，和北宋时期崛起的东莱吕氏世系完整衔接，形成了跨数百年的洛阳吕氏传承脉络。

第二阶段：五代至北宋 寿州吕氏的望族鼎盛

后唐时期家族成员吕梦奇官至御史中丞、户部侍郎，家族两支分别派生出洛阳与寿州两大支脉：

洛阳支由吕梦奇长子吕龟图延续，后续孕育出北宋名相吕蒙正，太平兴国二年状元及第，三次拜相封许国公，是宋代最负盛名的吕氏望族；
寿州支由吕梦奇次子吕龟祥延续，世代定居寿州形成寿州吕氏支派，和唐代吕温的河东本支形成一脉同源的传承关系。

吕蒙正之后，家族人才辈出，吕夷简、吕公著相继拜相，连续三代执掌北宋中枢，最终形成了被后世尊为“东莱吕氏”的庞大家族体系。

第三阶段：两宋之交 靖康南渡迁居婺州

北宋末年靖康之变，天下大乱，时任尚书右丞的东莱郡侯吕好问（字舜徒）率家族扈从宋高宗南渡，告别世居数百年的北方故土，落脚于婺州，完成了这支吕氏历史上第一次大规模跨江迁居。

这也是毗陵吕氏族谱1653年版原序中明确记载的：“徙居於吳越則自靖康南渡時舜徒公始遷婺州”的关键历史节点，家族自此扎根江南，不再返回北方旧籍。

第四阶段：南宋末年 自婺州迁居宜兴

南宋中后期，家族传人吕祖泰（字泰然）游历宜兴，十分喜爱当地山水清胜的环境，索性率族人迁居于此，正式在荆溪之畔定居下来，因此被族谱尊为迁宜兴的始祖。

自此这支吕氏在宜兴落地生根，繁衍数代，先后分化出荆溪渭渎分祖、荆溪双庙里分祖等多个分支，在太湖西岸形成了稳定的吕氏聚居群落。

第五阶段：明代初年 最终定居毗陵郡城

传到吕成（字春鹂）这一代时，其父吕能（字伯为）在洪武年间任都指挥，永乐三年因事被谪戍陕西卫所。吕成悲痛之下向官府泣请，自愿代父前往边地服役，最终孤身赴陕，不幸卒于戍所。

他的夫人杨氏此时带着两个儿子吕智、吕良，从宜兴启程迁居常州府城，被后世尊为毗陵吕氏的迁常始祖。杨氏携子定居后，将亡夫的遗骸安葬在郡城西门外怀南乡石塔新阡，墓地坐丙山壬向，家族自此在常州府城落地扎根，世代繁衍，最终形成了延续数百年的毗陵吕氏支派。

### Complete Migration Lineage of the Lü Clan

Combining the original records from the 1653 edition of Piling Lü Clan Genealogy and verifiable genealogical context of the distant ancestors of the Lü Clan, the centuries-long migration path of this Lü lineage is a typical epitome of the gradual southward spread of medieval scholar-official clans. The full historical process can be divided into five core stages:

Stage 1: From the Wei-Jin Period to the Prosperous Tang Dynasty – Establishment of the Foundation of the Hedong-Luoyang Lü Clan

The distant origin of the Piling Lü Clan can be traced back to the Hedong Lü lineage during the High Tang Dynasty. Its founding ancestor was Lü Yanzhi, who earned his jinshi (advanced scholar) degree in the 22nd year of the Kaiyuan era of the Tang Dynasty. He rose to the positions of Censor-in-Chief and Military Commissioner of Eastern Zhejiang Circuit, and his family had resided in the Hedong region for generations, making it one of the most prominent core branches of the Lü Clan in the Tang Dynasty.
Lü Yanzhi's son Lü Wei served as Vice Minister of Rites and Right Chief of the Crown Prince's Household, carrying forward the family's official and scholarly legacy. Lü Wei's son Lü Wen was even a renowned writer of the mid-Tang period, who reached the post of Prefectural Governor of Hengzhou, laying a profound foundation of prestige for the Hedong Lü Clan. This lineage later gradually moved to Luoyang, seamlessly connecting with the genealogy of the Donglai Lü Clan that rose to prominence in the Northern Song Dynasty, forming a Luoyang Lü Clan inheritance spanning hundreds of years.

Stage 2: From the Five Dynasties to the Northern Song Dynasty – The Heyday of the Shouzhou Lü Clan as a Prestigious Aristocratic Lineage

During the Later Tang Dynasty, a member of the clan named Lü Mengqi served as Censor-in-Chief and Vice Minister of Revenue. Two major branches derived from his line: the Luoyang branch and the Shouzhou branch.
The Luoyang branch was continued by Lü Guitu, the eldest son of Lü Mengqi, and later gave rise to Lü Mengzheng, the renowned chief minister of the Northern Song Dynasty. He topped the imperial examination as the zhuangyuan (top-ranked graduate) in the 2nd year of the Taiping Xingguo era, was appointed chief minister three times, and was enfeoffed as Duke of Xu, making him the most celebrated Lü Clan aristocrat of the Song Dynasty.
The Shouzhou branch was continued by Lü Guixiang, the second son of Lü Mengqi, who settled in Shouzhou for generations to form the Shouzhou Lü Clan branch, sharing the same origin as the Tang Dynasty Hedong main line led by Lü Wen. After Lü Mengzheng, the clan produced a steady stream of talented figures. Lü Yijian and Lü Gongzhe were successively appointed chief ministers, holding the central government power of the Northern Song for three consecutive generations, eventually forming the extensive clan system later respectfully referred to as the "Donglai Lü Clan".

Stage 3: The Turn of the Northern and Southern Song Dynasties – Moving to Wuzhou during the Jingkang Southward Exodus

At the end of the Northern Song Dynasty, the Jingkang Catastrophe plunged the realm into chaos. Lü Haowen (courtesy name Shuntu), who held the position of Right Vice Minister of the Department of State Affairs and was enfeoffed as Marquis of Donglai, led his clan to follow Emperor Gaozong of Song on his southward retreat. They bid farewell to their northern homeland where their family had lived for hundreds of years, and settled in Wuzhou, completing the first large-scale cross-Yangtze River migration in the history of this Lü lineage.
This is precisely the key historical node explicitly recorded in the original preface of the 1653 edition of Piling Lü Clan Genealogy: "The clan moved to the Wu-Yue region, which began when Lord Shuntu relocated to Wuzhou during the Jingkang Southward Exodus". From that point on, the clan took root in the Jiangnan (southern Yangtze River) region and never returned to their old northern homeland.

Stage 4: The Late Southern Song Dynasty – Moving from Wuzhou to Yixing

In the mid-to-late Southern Song Dynasty, Lü Zutai (courtesy name Tairan), a descendant of the clan, traveled to Yixing and was deeply captivated by the beautiful, serene landscape there. He simply led his clansmen to relocate to the area and officially settled along the banks of the Jingxi River, thus being respectfully regarded by the genealogy as the founding ancestor of the Yixing branch.
Since then, this Lü lineage put down roots in Yixing, multiplied for generations, and successively branched out into multiple sub-lineages such as the Jingxi Weidu Branch Ancestor and the Jingxi Shuangmiao Branch Ancestor, forming a stable Lü Clan settlement community on the west bank of Lake Tai.

Stage 5: The Early Ming Dynasty – Final Settlement in the Prefectural City of Pilin

By the generation of Lü Cheng (courtesy name Chunli), his father Lü Neng (courtesy name Bo Wei) served as a Regional Commander during the Hongwu era, but was exiled to a Shaanxi garrison due to an incident in the 3rd year of the Yongle era. Overwhelmed with grief, Lü Cheng tearfully pleaded with the local authorities, volunteering to take his father's place to serve in the frontier garrison. He ultimately traveled to Shaanxi alone and unfortunately died at his post.
At that time, Lü Cheng's wife Lady Yang set off from Yixing with her two sons Lü Zhi and Lü Liang, moving to the prefectural city of Changzhou. She was later respectfully honored as the founding ancestor of the Piling Lü Clan who first relocated to Changzhou. After settling down, Lady Yang buried the remains of her deceased husband at the new family cemetery in Shita, Huainan Township, outside the west gate of the prefectural city. The tomb was oriented with the Bing (south) mountain facing the Ren (north) direction. Since then, the clan took root in the prefectural city of Changzhou, multiplied for generations, and ultimately formed the Piling Lü Clan lineage that has lasted for hundreds of years.
