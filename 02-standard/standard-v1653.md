### 旧谱凡例 (1653)

* 作譜有尊祖敬宗之義焉吾呂氏自唐御史嵩山公至明都指揮伯為公得二十二世首遠祖表系示不敢忘也二十三世春鸝公自宜興遷武進為吾宗始遷祖今傳十六世表系另起詳列支派
* 吾呂氏世居河東宋東萊郡侯舜徒公當靖康之變扈從南來始居婺州至泰然公遊宜興愛山水之勝遂家焉此泰然公為遷宜興始祖而春鸝公為遷武進始祖世表下具詳焉
* 泰然公從兄諱祖謙諡成公之後分支由梁溪遷居郡城如前明仕至湖廣湖南道諱律今為北門分者皆公後也又泰然公後之現居宜興及春鸝公弟野鶴公諱咸子孫現居陽湖縣南鄉塘洋者俱各自有譜茲不具詳此譜止傳泰然公下春鸝公之支派至春鸝公次子友祥公舊譜載遷居江陰今未詳考故其支庶亦不及
* 吾家譜載泰然公四子而宜興譜止載二子曰繼宗紹宗名亦有異考其碑傳諸文並子列有子幾人名某名某墓誌墓碑中俱闕焉惟塘洋譜與吾家舊譜均載四子確然可信故自伯為公以上別列遠祖世表而泰然公四子諸名率由舊譜
* 始遷祖以下再傳而遷徙無考者為多至第五世始有三房今分三大分故世系圖中於第五世另起三分各自為祖詳其支派居徙大分則新閘南莊東莊二分則聚居郡城新廟巷至三分散處鄉城乡居如膠賢里洋蕩橋東車井小東門橋居城者惟太保公後俱於世表中標明條分縷晰瞭如指掌至太保公分長房內有寄居雲南又二三房後有寄籍廣東且莫詳其子若孫為何名者詢訪尚有待焉
* 蘇明允作族譜自其父上及高祖書曰諱某而他則質書之以為尊其所自出亦子孫秉筆不得不然之勢然蘇氏譜親盡則不及是直起於高祖耳故尊之無所嫌今吾譜自高祖而上至始遷祖尚得七世世代遙遠一概質書至族分既繁所居亦非一處名諱偶有相同疏遠者或聽之若本支有服之親則今卑幼更易以避尊長仍書原名於下嗣後命名宜詳查譜內不可再犯
* 家譜闕修幾及百年有生年卒葬多無考者執筆惘然不得已從闕疑之例其有考者則生卒之年月日時合葬分葬之墓穴山向均註明世表中其坐落鄉圖無號復於洞墓條內詳載之庶歷久無遺漏之憾其有各分查開未備僅取弗得者或從其闕至現在人丁生年月日時亦悉書之俾長幼秩然以便查考
* 凡妻黨之高曾祖父登科第有爵秩及雖無爵秩而族望可考者並書之於壻亦然使子孫無忘姻好也存者書名歿者書諱考核詳備又側室曾受誥封及有所出者書生年卒葬並同正嫡無所出則從略再醮來者書室不書配示有別也自我轉適者則缺其姓而不書賤失節也
* 無子者以兄弟之子為子仍於本生下敘明不沒始也表系不複書重為後也兼嗣則後書各重其為後也至乞養異姓子例不入譜然竟創而不載恐日久疑為遺漏轉致混淆故註明異姓子某而不列表系出嗣他族者列之表系俾不忘其本宗亦不致俁通婚嫁無子而未立嗣者註明無嗣世系中加以曲乙其舊譜未註明無嗣而現無支派接續者止註無考其未能確核尚待續查者註明俟考
* 封贈官階遵照賜軸書明其未受恩綸而應得請封者概稱例授例封例增無違於禮
* 凡稱某公若干世孫稱若干世祖概除本身而言如但言世次則自一世至五世如表系所稱者然
* 各祠依世次敘列馬山埠雖為太保公專祠係祀典稱高祖之父為五世祖元孫之子為五世孫是也其所關是宜首列至墳墓則有一塋而葬數世者難依世次均照表系各分序列焉
* 石塔祖塋自始遷祖以下共十四塚昭穆世次非圖不明故繪列於墓志之前以垂永久餘則繁不勝繪其有自願附圖出貲刊繪入譜備考者聽
* 先世所得勅書誥命敬載以誌寵榮至奏疏文翰繁多未付剞劂惟誌銘傳畧諸文所以表揚世德俱為編列托以紀實不事鋪張其無誌銘傳畧前此泯沒不彰者已多故凡見聞所及有才行可紀者皆累綴數言列為小傳彙於其次婦之苦節及女之守貞者亦各為敘畧附於小傳之次皆依世次長幼序列其已嫁而守節者應載所適之家譜內不贅
* 凡大節有虧不齒於族及曾為匪類者屏而不錄至盜鬻先塋地盜斫先塋樹盜賣宗祠祭產者蔑棄祖先莫此為甚公同拘至宗祠究追若桀驁不服控官處治族分長不得稍有徇隱
* 譜成共六十部編列號數領譜者註明某分某人領某號各宜珍藏每三年擇日傳知攜集公所查點損壞者罰跪一炷香遺失不全者罰修石塔墓將殘譜收回公所仍令追尋所失本數務在必得查到日交分長收貯有不肖售與同姓者察知會同族衆於各分公祠中重處追出仍令罰修祠墓
* 家譜所以收渙散奠世次關係匪輕今約族衆於每歲石塔集祭時各報一年消長之數及諸事件三分各置簿一本本支分長或管年者詳為載記十年編成一草譜三十年一續修則皆耳目聞見所及為功尤易焉

### 白话文 简体字 带标点

第一条：
编撰族谱有着尊崇祖先、敬重宗族的意义。我们吕氏家族从唐代的御史嵩山公开始，到明代的都指挥伯为公为止，共计二十二世。首先列出远祖的世系表，表示不敢忘记祖先。第二十三世春鹂公从宜兴迁徙到武进，是我们这一支宗族的始迁祖，到现在已经传了十六世。世系表从这里重新开始，详细列出各个支派。

第二条：
我们吕氏家族世代居住在河东。宋代东莱郡侯舜徒公在靖康之变时，护卫皇帝南渡，开始居住在婺州。后来泰然公游历宜兴，喜爱那里的山水胜景，于是定居下来。这位泰然公就是迁居宜兴的始祖，而春鹂公则是迁居武进的始祖，这些在世系表中都有详细记载。

第三条：
泰然公的堂兄名叫吕祖谦（谥号成公），他的后代分支从梁溪迁居到郡城（常州府城）。比如明代曾任湖广湖南道的吕律，以及现在被称为“北门分”的族人，都是他的后代。另外，泰然公的后代中现居宜兴的，以及春鹂公的弟弟野鹤公（名咸）的子孙现居阳湖县南乡塘洋的，都有各自的族谱，这里就不详细记载了。本谱只记载泰然公之下、春鹂公这一支派。至于春鹂公的次子友祥公，旧谱记载他迁居江阴，现在无法详细考证，所以他的后代也就不收录了。

第四条：
我们家的旧谱记载泰然公有四个儿子，而宜兴的族谱只记载了两个，叫继宗、绍宗，名字也有差异。考证碑传等文章以及儿子列表中关于有几个儿子、叫什么名字的记载，在墓志铭和墓碑中都缺失了。只有塘洋谱和我们家的旧谱都记载是四个儿子，确实可信。所以从伯为公以上另外列出远祖世系表，而泰然公四个儿子的名字主要依据旧谱。

第五条：
始迁祖以下再传一两代就迁徙且无法考证的情况很多，直到第五世才开始分为三房，现在分为三大分支。所以在世系图中，从第五世开始另起三支，各自作为祖先，详细记载他们的支派和迁徙居住地。大分支居住在新闸、南庄、东庄；二分支聚居在郡城新庙巷；三分支散处在城乡各地，乡居比如在胶贤里、洋荡桥、东车井、小东门桥，居住在城里的只有太保公的后代。这些都在世系表中标明，条理清晰，一目了然。至于太保公长房内有寄居云南的，又有二三房后代寄籍广东的，而且不知道他们的子孙叫什么名字，还需要进一步寻访调查。

第六条：
苏洵（苏明允）编写族谱时，从父亲往上到高祖都写上“讳某”（表示避讳），其他的则直接写名字，这是为了尊崇自己的直系祖先，也是子孙执笔不得不如此的惯例。但是苏氏谱亲属关系尽了就不再记载，直接从高祖开始，所以尊崇直系没有嫌疑。现在我们这部谱从高祖往上到始迁祖还有七世，年代久远，一律直接写名字。等到家族分支繁多，居住地也不在一处，名字偶尔有相同的，疏远的族人或许可以听之任之；如果是本支有服丧关系的亲属，现在的晚辈就要改名以避开长辈的名讳，并在下面写上原名。以后取名应该详细查阅族谱，不可再犯忌讳。

第七条：
族谱缺修将近百年，许多人的生年、卒年、葬地都无法考证，执笔编写时感到茫然，不得已只能遵循“阙疑”（存疑不录）的原则。有考证清楚的，则将生卒年月日时、合葬或分葬的墓穴朝向都在世系表中注明。如果坐落地点在乡图中没有编号，就在“茔墓”条目内详细记载，希望历经长久也没有遗漏的遗憾。如果有各分支查报不全、暂时无法获取信息的，或者空缺的，直到现在的丁口生年月日时也全部写上，使长幼有序，方便查考。

第八条：
凡是妻子娘家的高祖、曾祖、祖父、父亲，如果考取了功名、有官爵品级，或者虽然没有官爵但家族声望可考的，都要记录下来；女婿的情况也是如此，让子孙不要忘记姻亲关系。活着的人写名字，去世的人写讳名，考核要详细完备。另外，侧室（妾）如果曾经受过朝廷诰封或者有生育子女的，要记载生卒葬地，待遇同正妻一样；没有生育子女的就从略记载。改嫁来的妇女写“室”不写“配”，以示区别。自己转嫁他人的则空缺她的姓氏不予记载，这是鄙视她失节。

第九条：
没有儿子的人，以兄弟的儿子为子，仍然在他原本的生父名下叙述明白，不埋没他的出身。在世系表中不再重复书写，是为了重视过继关系。如果是兼祧（一人继承两房香火），则在后面分别书写，重视他继承两边的身份。至于乞养异姓儿子，按惯例不入族谱，但如果完全删除不记载，恐怕时间久了会被误认为是遗漏，反而导致混淆，所以注明是异姓子某某，但不列入正式世系表。过继给其他族人的，列入世系表，让他不忘本宗，也不至于导致通婚嫁娶的错误。没有儿子且未立嗣的，注明“无嗣”，在世系中加以标记（曲乙符号）。旧谱未注明无嗣而现在没有支派接续的，只注“无考”。那些未能确切核实、尚待继续查访的，注明“俟考”。

第十条：
封赠的官阶遵照皇帝赐下的轴书（诰命文书）写明。那些没有受到皇帝恩纶但实际上应该得到请封的，一概称为“例授”、“例封”、“例增”，这并不违背礼制。

第十一条：
凡是称呼某公为若干世孙、称呼若干世祖，大概都是除去本身而言的。比如只说世次，就是从一世到五世，如同世系表中所称呼的那样。

第十二条：
各祠堂依照世次排列。马山埠虽然是太保公的专祠，属于祭祀典礼的一部分（这里举例解释世次计算：称高祖的父亲为五世祖，玄孙的儿子为五世孙，就是这个道理）。这就关系到应该把祠堂放在首位。至于坟墓，有同一座坟茔埋葬了几代人的情况，很难完全依照世次排列，都按照世系表各分支的顺序排列。

第十三条：
石塔祖坟从始迁祖以下共有十四座坟墓，昭穆世次如果不是画图就不清楚，所以绘制图表列在墓志之前，以垂永久。其余的坟墓太多无法一一绘制，如果有自愿附图出资刊印载入族谱以备查考的，听任其便。

第十四条：
祖先所得到的敕书、诰命恭敬地记载下来，以记录皇恩荣耀。至于奏疏、文章太多，没有交付刻印，只有志铭、传略等文章是用来表扬世代德行的，都编列进来，以此纪实，不事铺张。那些没有志铭传略、此前埋没不显扬的人已经很多了，所以凡是见闻所及、有才能品行值得记录的，都连缀数语列为小传，汇集在后面。妇女的苦节以及女儿的守贞，也各自写个简略附在小传后面，都依照世次长幼排列。已经出嫁而守节的，应该记载在她丈夫家的族谱内，这里不再赘述。

第十五条：
凡是大节有亏、被家族不齿的人，以及曾经做过匪类的人，屏除不予记录。至于盗卖祖坟地、盗砍祖坟树、盗卖宗祠祭产的人，蔑弃祖先没有比这更严重的了，族人应共同将其拘捕到宗祠追究追讨；如果桀骜不服，就控告官府处治，族分长不得稍有徇私隐瞒。

第十六条：
族谱修成共六十部，编列号码。领谱的人注明某分某人领某号，各自应当珍藏。每三年选择日子通知大家携带族谱到公所查点。损坏的人罚跪一炷香的时间；遗失不全的人罚修石塔墓，并将残谱收回公所，仍命令追寻丢失的本数，务必找回。查到后交给分长收贮。如果有不肖子孙卖给同姓外人的，察觉后会同族众在各分公祠中从重处罚，追回族谱，并仍令罚修祠墓。

第十七条：
族谱是用来收拢涣散的族人、奠定世系次序的，关系非常重大。现在约定族众在每年石塔集祭的时候，各自报告一年的人口增减数目及各种事件。三个分支各置一本簿子，由本支分长或管年的人详细记载。十年编成一部草谱，三十年进行一次续修，这样都是耳目闻见所及的事情，做起来尤其容易。

### The Writing Standards (1653)

Article 1:
The compilation of a genealogy embodies the principles of honoring ancestors and respecting the clan. Our Lü clan, from the Tang Dynasty Censor Lord Songshan (嵩山公) to the Ming Dynasty Commander Lord Bowei (伯為公) in the 22nd generation, lists the ancestral lineage first to show that we dare not forget. In the 23rd generation, Lord Chunli (春鸝公) moved from Yixing to Wujin, becoming the founding ancestor of our branch. Now transmitted for 16 generations, the lineage chart starts anew here, detailing the branches.

Article 2:
Our Lü clan has always lived in Hedong. During the Jingkang Incident in the Song Dynasty, Lord Shuntu (舜徒公), the Marquis of Donglai, followed the imperial court south and first settled in Wuzhou. Later, Lord Tairan (泰然公) traveled to Yixing, loved the beauty of its mountains and waters, and settled there. Thus, Lord Tairan is the founding ancestor of the Yixing branch, while Lord Chunli is the founding ancestor of the Wujin branch. These details are fully provided in the lineage tables.

Article 3:
After Lord Tairan's cousin, Lord Zuqian (祖謙, posthumously Lord Cheng), his descendants branched off and moved from Liangxi to the prefecture city. For example, Lord Lü (諱律), who served as the Hunan Circuit Commissioner in the Ming Dynasty, and those now known as the "North Gate Branch," are all his descendants. Additionally, the descendants of Lord Tairan currently living in Yixing, and the descendants of Lord Chunli's younger brother, Lord Yehe (野鶴公, name Cheng), currently living in Tangyang, Nanxiang, Yanghu County, all have their own genealogies, which are not detailed here. This genealogy only transmits the branch of Lord Chunli under Lord Tairan. As for Lord Chunli's second son, Lord Youxiang (友祥公), the old genealogy records that he moved to Jiangyin, but this has not been verified in detail, so his descendants are not included.

Article 4:
Our family genealogy records that Lord Tairan had four sons, while the Yixing genealogy only records two, named Jizong (繼宗) and Shaozong (紹宗), and the names also differ. Examining various texts such as stele inscriptions and biographies, as well as lists of sons mentioning how many sons there were and their names, these are all missing from tombstone inscriptions and epitaphs. Only the Tangyang genealogy and our old family genealogy both record four sons, which is credible. Therefore, the distant ancestral lineage is listed separately above Lord Bowei, while the names of Lord Tairan's four sons follow the old genealogy.

Article 5:
There are many cases where descendants of the founding ancestor moved in the subsequent generations without a trace. It was not until the fifth generation that three branches emerged, which are now divided into three major branches. Therefore, in the lineage chart, three branches are started separately in the fifth generation, each serving as an ancestor, detailing their branches and places of residence. The major branch is in Xinzha (新閘), Nanzhuang (南莊), and Dongzhuang (東莊); the second branch is concentrated in Xinmiao Lane (新廟巷) in the prefecture city; the third branch is scattered between the countryside and the city, with rural residences in places like Jiaoxianli (膠賢里), Yangdang Bridge (洋蕩橋), Dongchejing (東車井), and Xiaodongmen Bridge (小東門橋), while only the descendants of Lord Taibao (太保公) live in the city. These are all clearly marked in the lineage table, distinct and clear as if pointing at one's palm. As for the branch of Lord Taibao, there are those in the long house living in Yunnan, and later generations of the second and third houses living in Guangdong, whose children and grandchildren's names are unknown. Inquiries are still pending.

Article 6:
When Su Mingyun (蘇明允) compiled his clan genealogy, he wrote "taboo name" (諱某) for his father and up to his great-grandfather, while writing the names of others plainly. This was to honor his direct ancestors, an inevitable trend when descendants hold the pen. However, the Su clan genealogy does not go beyond the great-grandfather, starting directly from there, so there is no suspicion in honoring them. Now, our genealogy goes back seven generations from the great-grandfather to the founding ancestor. The generations are distant, so all names are written plainly. Since the clan branches are numerous and live in different places, names occasionally coincide. For distant relatives, this may be overlooked, but for close relatives within the mourning period, the younger generation must change their names to avoid those of the elders, while still recording the original name below. Future naming should carefully check the genealogy to avoid repetition.

Article 7:
The genealogy has not been revised for nearly a hundred years, so the birth and death years and burial places of many are unknown. Holding the pen in confusion, we have no choice but to follow the principle of omitting doubtful matters. For those that can be verified, the year, month, day, and hour of birth and death, as well as the grave location and orientation for joint or separate burials, are all clearly noted in the lineage table. For those whose village or plot number is unknown, they are detailed in the tomb section to prevent future omissions. For branches that have not been fully investigated or where information could not be obtained, we omit them. The birth dates of current members are also fully recorded to maintain order and facilitate verification.

Article 8:
For the fathers, grandfathers, and great-grandfathers of wives who passed imperial examinations or held official ranks, and even those without ranks but with verifiable clan prestige, their names are recorded. The same applies to sons-in-law, so that descendants do not forget their marital ties. For the living, names are recorded; for the deceased, taboo names are used, with detailed verification. Additionally, for concubines who received imperial titles or bore children, their birth and death dates and burials are recorded the same as legitimate wives; those without children are omitted. Women who remarried are recorded as "chamber" (室) but not "spouse" (配) to show the distinction. Those who left our clan to remarry are not recorded with their surnames, as they are considered to have lost their virtue.

Article 9:
Those without sons adopt the sons of brothers, which is still recorded under their own birth entries to not erase their origin. They are not repeated in the lineage chart to emphasize they are successors. Those who inherit two lines are recorded under both to emphasize their dual succession. As for adopted sons of different surnames, they are generally not included in the genealogy. However, completely deleting them might lead to confusion or suspicion of omission in the future. Therefore, they are noted as "adopted son of surname X" without a place in the lineage chart. Those who go to other clans are listed in the chart so they do not forget their origin and to prevent accidental intermarriage. Those without sons who have not established an heir are marked as "no heir" with a curve in the chart. If the old genealogy did not specify "no heir" but there are currently no continuing branches, it is marked as "unknown." Those that cannot be verified are marked as "pending verification."

Article 10:
Official ranks and titles follow the imperial edicts. Those who have not received an imperial decree but are eligible for a title are generally referred to as "appointed by regulation" (例授), "enfeoffed by regulation" (例封), or "posthumously honored by regulation" (例贈), without violating etiquette.

Article 11:
When referring to a lord as an ancestor of a certain generation or a descendant of a certain generation, it generally excludes oneself. For example, the father of a great-grandfather is the fifth-generation ancestor, and the son of a great-grandson is the fifth-generation descendant. If only the generation number is mentioned, it refers to the sequence from the first to the fifth generation as shown in the lineage chart.

Article 12:
Ancestral halls are arranged according to generational order. Although the Mashan Port hall is a special shrine for Lord Taibao and is related to sacrificial rites, it should be listed first. As for graves, some tombs have buried multiple generations, making it difficult to follow the generational order strictly, so they are sequenced according to the lineage chart branches.

Article 13:
The ancestral graveyard at Shita (Stone Pagoda) has a total of 14 tombs from the founding ancestor onwards. The generational order of seniority is unclear without a diagram, so they are drawn and listed before the tomb records to preserve them permanently. The rest are too numerous to draw. Those who voluntarily wish to attach a diagram and pay for its engraving in the genealogy for reference may do so.

Article 14:
Imperial edicts and titles obtained by ancestors are respectfully recorded to mark the imperial favor. As for memorials and literary works, they are too numerous to be engraved. Only epitaphs and brief biographies that praise ancestral virtues are compiled to record facts without exaggeration. For those without epitaphs or biographies who have been obscure, many are now included if their deeds are known, with a few sentences added to form a short biography, compiled in order. Women who endured hardship and daughters who remained chaste are also briefly described and attached to the biographies, sequenced by generation and age. Those who remained chaste after marriage should be recorded in their husband's family genealogy and are not repeated here.

Article 15:
Those who have committed major moral offenses and are unworthy of the clan, as well as those who have been criminals, are excluded and not recorded. As for those who sell ancestral grave land, cut down ancestral grave trees, or embezzle ancestral hall sacrificial properties, they show the utmost disrespect to ancestors. They will be publicly detained in the ancestral hall for investigation and repayment. If they are stubborn and refuse to comply, they will be reported to the authorities for punishment. Branch heads must not show any favoritism or concealment.

Article 16:
A total of 60 copies of the genealogy have been completed, numbered sequentially. The recipient of each copy is noted with their branch and name. Each copy should be treasured. Every three years, a date will be chosen to gather at the public hall for inspection. Those who damage the book will be punished by kneeling for the duration of burning one stick of incense. Those who lose it or have an incomplete copy will be punished by repairing the Stone Pagoda grave. Damaged copies will be returned to the public hall, and the owner will be ordered to retrieve the missing volumes, with success being mandatory. Upon retrieval, they will be handed over to the branch head for storage. Any unworthy person selling a copy to someone of the same surname will be severely punished in the ancestral hall upon discovery, and the book will be recovered. They will also be ordered to pay for the repair of the ancestral hall and grave.

Article 17:
The genealogy serves to gather the scattered and establish the generational order, which is of great importance. It is now agreed that at the annual Stone Pagoda sacrificial gathering, each branch will report the births and deaths of the past year and other events. Three separate account books will be kept, recorded in detail by the branch heads or annual managers. A draft genealogy will be compiled every ten years, and a full revision every thirty years. Since these are based on what is seen and heard, the work will be much easier.
