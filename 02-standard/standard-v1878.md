### 光緒四年增修凡例 (1878)

* 舊譜分十卷首世系次世表次恩綸次冢墓次文傳次小傳次附記此次世衍丁繁卷帙日富因分為一十八卷次第一遵前例惟將文傳內內行傳誌另彙一卷為內傳又易附記為附譜其中簡帙繁多者分上中下卷世表行第字號科名官職封典生卒葬所妻室子女及有傳銘行署并行誼載入志乘者其行款胥循舊譜
* 舊譜世系倣蘇氏法累代相繼長則銜接本宗次則順列於後萬派一源瞭如指掌惟自始遷至今世歷十八而舊圖以三大分祖起行每行十三世字已甚密不能再增況以後生齒日繁奕葉遞衍自應稍變其體因酌定十世一行行末標明子幾人某某其繼與別房者不敘以歸簡省十一世上標明某人第幾子係立嗣者則稱嗣子遞相銜接仍分三分各敘支派秩然
* 舊譜世表倣歐陽體五世一行其五世另起處標明某人子不用復見以就簡省惟吾族自春鸝公始遷第三世有兄弟四人而華觀傑三公後皆不傳至第五世始分三大分今在毘陵者皆此三分子孫也支庶日繁幾於莫可辨皙故酌於世表中第五世三大分祖另起複見以下各分分錄仍循舊例庶宗派益覺分明
* 齒為達尊同於爵德故六十以上稱壽並詳其歲數亦古人引年之義也殤者不載其有年已成童及本支無後有待議嗣者仍行載入又舊譜有名旋即夭折者亦存之以備查考
* 舊譜中名諱有與廟諱御名同字不問久遠一律謹避用同音字代之仍註明於下
* 舊譜內所有墳圖此次均為附刊以垂永久現在各房子孫有願繪圖增入者均令各自捐貲刊刻其塋地坐落都圖村鎮字號畝分兆域山向墓田均照舊例詳敘彙入祠墓一門無考及漏未開送者闕
* 舊譜於祝子贅婿概不列入世次第於表系內註明惟其中有無宗可歸歷久仍繫吾姓者自應量為變通彙集譜後名曰附譜俾合族咸悉其來源所自以廣收族之仁即以杜冒宗之漸至表系內仍註明乞養子以符舊例亦不至於混淆
* 吾族宜興始祖為宋迪功郎泰然公而宜興譜與吾譜不符已詳舊例現有春鸝公弟懋溪公盛子孫現居荊溪渭瀆其譜亦與吾譜脗合世遠年湮無從確考自當各守傳聞異辭之義以示率由
* 吾族宗譜創自順治十年距始遷時已遠自乾隆辛亥輯修又越百數十年今又五十載矣分處鄉邑散失無考者不可勝數上次修譜時即有閔黃大樹基村等處赴局請修以推按舊譜不甚相符因作附記一卷詳其支系以俟詳覈今復有奔牛鎮後等支亦皆無宗派可接錄其世次歸入附譜以從闕疑之義其舊譜附記今未開送者亦綴數行以附於後
* 此次修譜仍照舊章各分捐下彙辦其有不敷係族中在外作宦遊幕者勉力捐輸另刻捐錄隨譜給發
* 此次舊譜編某人收領字樣號分給收領譜後列明第幾號某分印共一百部循以備續修查繳
* 出身官階仍照舊譜敘次凡從前及此次開送來局有假借者悉遵令典改正其不合者概從刪除
* 凡書例授例晉字樣無論京外官惟業經到署到任未經請封而應得封贈及已升缺應得晉封者始照官階書之未出仕而僅有職銜者概不書
* 妻若婿之祖父敘其出身及最後官職無官職書出身顯名者或書一代而止間有封贈顯職亦書之世遠年湮冀有諮考以通姻好非事鋪張也比封贈官階概不書於婿亦然
* 舊譜無嗣者悉註出今有久沒遠省者其有關與否未盡詳悉又在里者亂後被擄未歸均未便加以無嗣字樣故刪去以歸一律有書嗣無考者仍之蓋有嗣而無考所以別於無嗣也
* 舊譜於生卒年月或書未詳或書俟考此次續修計二百年來迄無考證不必再俟悉書無考二字惟舊有生率而未全者仍之又此次增入生卒無考者生年間空格卒年則以卒字畢之
* 所配守節無論請旌與否其事跡已見內傳列女傳及崇祀門世表書僅旌表祀祠字樣均不復贅題請亦有所考證也惟年例未符而又無傳載者則當稍敘其略俾他年
* 咸豐庚申之亂合族殉難者甚多均採訪確實世表中註殉難二字并查照邑志其已題請入祀忠義祠男註旌卹視忠義祠婦註旌表祀節烈祠而敘其事略於崇祀門有男人係被擄者暫書被擄二字
* 舊譜凡有傳誌者世表悉註明其僅見小傳者不註今凡載在小傳及列女傳者悉註見某傳
* 凡婚嫁之家在他府縣者註明某縣不同省者註明某省
* 凡女之未適人而有守貞旌表者亦於其父之世表中註明
* 此次修譜有各分從前出外迄今不知踪跡存亡莫考者十餘支悉註出外無考用是惴惴今議現散處各省者及今有方可稽無論宦遊及流寓概註明現在某省或某縣以備他年收族之效舊譜太保公後二三房寄居雲南廣東兩省者悉為標明今仍之亦此意也以上世表
* 比庚申殉難男婦別立崇祀一門分忠義節烈兩祠其旌卹年次并查照邑志詳敘號志願者皆標明按世數編列以彰國典而彰忠節凡前令曾入名宦昭忠節孝諸祠之散見於譜中者悉為採列并稍敘事略以太保公專祠冠首所部士民公建之祠亦附焉
* 凡節婦請旌年次舊譜未詳者悉據邑志查明備載又從前有苦節而未旌者亦將邑志完節未旌類并近年守節者以次探入節孝祠後可備他年補請旌表
* 舊譜事蹟載國史及府縣志者僅於世表註明此次悉為採入編傳狀第一卷前為上卷
* 各類排次據世數外復考其年齒長幼同庚則據月之前後婦則就夫之年為次惟殉難中多有生年無考者則但就三大分之世數敘列焉
* 科目向不另敘茲照志書體例由貢生至進士按科分附載恩綸志後所以紀國恩而思祖德不第為志乘光也
* 各分塋墓其子孫有自願繪圖者仍照舊章另行捐資刻入凡新增傳文誌銘亦如之
* 舊載祠墓田產無細數業戶亂後田地減數甚多無從查考今悉照聯單分別條記無單者列入下卷
* 舊譜凡有封贈賜軸悉刻入恩綸志此次有誥敕曾頒而遭亂遺失難於搜採或住宦在外催取勿得者均照官階約記年月附於後示不敢忘國恩也
* 凡有遺行可傳或無嗣或子孫未及纂述者悉採摭事實為之立傳與各分子孫纂述及請人所作墓銘家傳一併按次編入客者列入小*傳
* 凡節婦事親撫孤垂數十年而無專傳所有事實悉採訪詳明循舊譜例略為敘述載入列女傳他如賢母孝婦未嫁烈女以及妾媵有賢行者亦並得書不沒其實也
* 舊以軼支與養子歸入附譜位置得宜令子姓日繁因分軼支另為一卷又有支派未一族情未洽者苦支簡端心參酌特以此類附後黃韻專以養子歸入附譜
* 凡軼支與附譜有事實可傳者亦分別採附各類後不令向隅且以見祖德之入人深而感孚者廣也
* 凡傳狀中標目悉從公共稱謂此舊例也今遵之
* 舊譜十八卷今次第悉遵前例惟以原序及新舊凡例祖訓宗約目錄等為卷首增崇祠一卷分祠墓一卷為兩卷又各房增刻傳狀一卷別傳狀中碑銘為一卷分附譜中軼支為第二十二卷而編附譜為卷末計共二十四卷
* 舊譜偶有亥豕之誤及表傳生卒年齒不符處今悉詳考一一校正然此次各類皆係一人手輯校讎又無專司難保舊譜之舛誤未盡新修之舛誤又增其體例兼有未歸一律者是在後君子加意參訂匡其不逮
* 此次刷譜共六十部編號分給用帳隨發悉遵舊規

### 白话文 简体字 带标点

第一条
旧谱分为十卷，顺序依次是：世系图、世系表、恩纶（皇帝诏令）、冢墓（坟墓）、文传、小传、附记。这次因为子孙繁衍，卷帙日益丰富，所以分为十八卷。次序大致遵循旧例，只是将文传中的女性传记单独汇编为一卷，称为“内传”；又将“附记”改名为“附谱”。其中篇幅繁多的部分，分为上、中、下三卷。世系表中的排行、字号、科名、官职、封典、生卒年月、葬地、妻室子女，以及有传记铭文、事迹载入地方志的，其格式都遵循旧谱。

第二条
旧谱的世系图仿照苏洵（苏氏）的方法，代代相继，长房衔接本宗，次房顺列于后，万派一源，一目了然。只是从始迁祖到现在已经经历了十八代，旧图从三大分支的祖先开始排列，每行十三代，字迹已经非常密集，无法再增加。况且以后人口日益繁多，枝叶递衍，自然应该稍微改变体例。因此斟酌定为每行十代，行末标明有几个儿子、名字叫什么，那些过继给别房的不再叙述，以求简省。十一世以上标明某人的第几个儿子，如果是立嗣的则称为“嗣子”，递相衔接，仍然分为三个分支，各自叙述支派，井然有序。

第三条
旧谱的世系表仿照欧阳修（欧阳体）的体例，五世一行，在五世另起的地方标明是谁的儿子，不再重复出现，以求简省。只是我族自从春鹂公始迁以来，第三世有兄弟四人，而华、观、杰三公的后代都没有传承，到第五世才分为三大分支，现在在毗陵的都是这三公的子孙。支庶日益繁多，几乎难以分辨清楚，所以斟酌在世系表中，将第五世的三大分支祖先另起一行重复出现，以下各分支分别记录，仍然遵循旧例，希望宗派更加分明。

第四条
年龄是为了表示尊重，与爵位德行同等重要，所以六十岁以上称为“寿”，并详细记载岁数，这也是古人尊老的意义。未成年夭折的不记载，如果有已经成年（成童）以及本支没有后代、有待商议立嗣的，仍然载入。另外，旧谱中有名字刚取就夭折的，也保留下来以备查考。

第五条
旧谱中的名讳如果有与皇帝庙讳、御名相同的字，不管年代多久远，一律谨慎避讳，用同音字代替，并在下面注明原字。

第六条
旧谱内所有的坟墓图，这次都作为附录刊印，以垂永久。现在各房子孙有愿意绘图增入的，都让他们各自出资刊刻。墓地的坐落、都图、村镇、字号、亩分、兆域、山向、墓田，都照旧例详细叙述，汇入“祠墓”一门。无法考证及遗漏未报送的，则空缺。

第七条
旧谱对于义子（祝子）、赘婿一概不列入世系表，只在表系内注明。只是其中有些人没有宗族可归，历久仍然系我吕姓，自然应该量情变通，汇集在谱后，名叫“附谱”，使全族都知道他们的来源，以推广收族的仁心，也就是为了杜绝冒认宗族的渐端。至于在表系内仍然注明是“乞养子”，以符合旧例，也不至于混淆。

第八条
我族宜兴始祖是宋代的迪功郎泰然公，而宜兴谱与我们的谱不符，已在旧例中详细说明。现有春鹂公的弟弟懋溪公（名盛）的子孙，现居荆溪渭渎，他们的谱也与我们的谱吻合。因为年代久远，无从确考，自然应当各自遵守传闻异辞的原则，以示遵循旧制。

第九条
我族宗谱创修于顺治十年，距离始迁时已经很远了。自从乾隆辛亥年辑修后，又过了一百几十年，现在又过了五十年。分散在各乡邑、散失无法考证的不可胜数。上次修谱时，就有闵黄、大树基村等处的人来局请求修谱，因为推算旧谱不太相符，所以作了一卷“附记”，详细记录他们的支系，以待详细核实。现在又有奔牛镇后等支派，也都无宗派可接，记录他们的世次归入附谱，以遵从存疑的原则。旧谱附记中现在未报送的，也缀写几行附在后面。

第十条
这次修谱仍然照旧章程，各分支捐款汇总办理。如果有不够的，由族中在外做官或做幕僚的人勉力捐助，另外刻印“捐录”随谱发给。

第十一条
这次旧谱编写了某人收领的字样，编号分给收领，谱后列明第几号、某分，共印一百部，遵循旧规以备续修时查缴。

第十二条
出身官阶仍然照旧谱叙述次序。凡是从前及这次报送到局里有假借官衔的，都遵照法令改正；不合规定的，一概删除。

第十三条
凡是书写“例授”、“例晋”字样，无论京官外官，只有已经到署到任、未经请封而应得封赠，以及已升缺应得晋封的，才照官阶书写。未出仕而仅有职衔的一概不书写。

第十四条
妻子或女婿的祖父，叙述其出身及最后官职；无官职的书写出身；显名的或者只写一代为止。间或有封赠显职的也书写。因为年代久远，希望能有咨询考证以通姻亲友好，并非为了铺张。比如封赠官阶一概不书写，对女婿也是如此。

第十五条
旧谱中无嗣的都注明“出”（指出继或外出）。现在有久没于远省的，其有无后代未尽详悉；又有在乡里的人，乱后被掳未归，都不便加以“无嗣”字样，所以删去以归一律。有书写“嗣无考”的保留它，因为有嗣而无考，是用来区别于无嗣的。

第十六条
旧谱对于生卒年月，有的写“未详”，有的写“俟考”。这次续修，计算二百年来迄无考证，不必再等，一律书写“无考”二字。只有旧谱有生卒而未全的，仍然保留。又这次增入生卒无考的

### The Writing Standards (1878)

Article 1:
The old genealogy was divided into ten volumes: first the lineage charts, then the lineage tables, imperial honors, tombs, biographies, short biographies, and appendices. This time, due to the proliferation of descendants and the increasing volume of records, it has been expanded to eighteen volumes. The order generally follows the old rules, except that the biographies of women's conduct have been compiled into a separate volume called "Inner Biographies," and the "Appendix" has been renamed "Supplementary Genealogy." Sections with extensive content are divided into upper, middle, and lower volumes. In the lineage tables, details such as birth order, style names, examination titles, official positions, honors, dates of birth and death, burial places, wives, and children, as well as those with biographies or local chronicle records, follow the formatting of the old genealogy.

Article 2:
The lineage chart of the old genealogy followed the Su Shi method, with generations connected in succession. The eldest branch connected to the main clan, and the younger branches were listed in order, making the single origin of all branches clear. However, from the first migrant ancestor to the present, eighteen generations have passed. The old charts started from the ancestors of the three major branches, with thirteen generations per row, and the text had become too dense to add more. Moreover, with the daily increase in population and the continuous branching out, a change in format was necessary. Therefore, it has been decided to list ten generations per row, marking the number of sons and their names at the end of each row. Those adopted into other branches are not listed to maintain simplicity. From the eleventh generation onwards, it is marked as "so-and-so's nth son," and if adopted, they are called "adopted son." They are connected in sequence, still divided into three branches, each with its own orderly lineage.

Article 3:
The lineage table of the old genealogy followed the Ouyang Xiu style, with five generations per row. At the start of a new five-generation block, the father's name was not repeated to save space. However, for our clan, starting from the migrant ancestor Lord Chunli, there were four brothers in the third generation, but the descendants of Lords Hua, Guan, and Jie were not transmitted. It was not until the fifth generation that the three major branches emerged, and all current members in Piling are descendants of these three. As the branches have become increasingly complex and difficult to distinguish, it has been decided to repeat the ancestors of the three major branches at the fifth generation in the lineage table, and then list each branch separately below, following the old rules, so that the branches are even clearer.

Article 4:
Age is a mark of respect equal to rank and virtue, so those over sixty are referred to as having "longevity," and their exact ages are detailed, following the ancient principle of honoring the elderly. Those who die young are not recorded, but those who have reached adulthood (15 years old) or those whose branches have no descendants and are awaiting adoption are still included. Additionally, names of those who died shortly after birth in the old genealogy are retained for reference.

Article 5:
If any names in the old genealogy are identical to the imperial names or the names of emperors in the ancestral temple, they must be strictly avoided regardless of how long ago they were. Homophones are used as substitutes, and the original characters are noted below.

Article 6:
All tomb diagrams in the old genealogy are published as supplements this time to preserve them permanently. Descendants of all branches who wish to add diagrams must donate funds for engraving. Details such as the location, administrative division, village, plot number, size, boundaries, orientation, and tomb fields are recorded in detail according to the old rules and compiled into the "Ancestral Halls and Tombs" section. Information that cannot be verified or was not submitted is left blank.

Article 7:
Adopted sons (Zhuzi) and sons-in-law living with the family (Zhuixu) are generally not included in the lineage tables in the old genealogy, but only noted within them. However, for those who have no clan to return to and have long borne our surname, a flexible approach is taken by compiling them at the end of the genealogy under the name "Supplementary Genealogy." This allows the whole clan to know their origins, extending the benevolence of gathering the clan while preventing the gradual encroachment of false claims. In the lineage tables, they are still marked as "adopted sons" to conform to the old rules and avoid confusion.

Article 8:
Our clan's ancestor in Yixing was Lord Tairan, a Diligence Gentleman of the Song Dynasty. The Yixing genealogy does not match ours, as detailed in the old rules. Currently, the descendants of Lord Maoxi (name Sheng), the younger brother of Lord Chunli, live in Weidu, Jingxi. Their genealogy matches ours, but due to the great distance in time and lack of definitive verification, we should each adhere to the principle of "hearsay differs" to show our compliance with the old ways.

Article 9:
Our clan's genealogy was first compiled in the tenth year of Shunzhi, long after the initial migration. Since the revision in the Xinhai year of Qianlong, another hundred years have passed, and now another fifty years have gone by. Many branches have scattered and been lost. During the last revision, branches from Minhuang, Dashuji, and other villages came to the bureau requesting revisions, but their connection to the old genealogy was unclear, so an appendix volume was made to detail their branches for future verification. Now, branches from Bengniu Town and other places also have no connectable lineage; their generations are recorded in the Supplementary Genealogy to follow the principle of leaving doubts unresolved. Those not submitted from the old appendix are also briefly added at the end.

Article 10:
This revision follows the old regulations, with each branch donating funds for compilation. Any shortfall is covered by the generous donations of clan members serving as officials or secretaries outside, and a record of donations is printed and distributed with the genealogy.

Article 11:
This time, the old genealogies are numbered and distributed to specific individuals for safekeeping. The number and branch are listed at the end of the book. A total of 100 copies are printed to facilitate future revisions and collection.

Article 12:
Damaged copies will result in a penalty of kneeling for the duration of an incense stick. Lost or incomplete copies will incur a fine for repairing the Shita tomb, and the damaged genealogy will be confiscated by the clan hall. The lost copies must be recovered. If a copy is found to be sold to someone of the same surname, the offender will be severely punished in the ancestral hall, the book recovered, and a fine imposed for repairing the ancestral hall and tomb.

Article 13:
The genealogy serves to gather the scattered and establish the generational order, which is no light matter. It is now proposed that at the annual gathering for worship at the Shita tomb, each branch reports the number of births and deaths and other events for the year. Each of the three branches will keep a register, recorded in detail by the branch head or the annual manager. Every ten years, a draft genealogy will be compiled, and every thirty years, a full revision will be made. This way, the work will be easy as it relies on direct knowledge and observation.

Article 14:
Official ranks and titles follow the old genealogy's order. Any fake titles claimed in the past or submitted this time will be corrected according to official regulations, and those that do not conform will be deleted.

Article 15:
Regarding the notation of "appointed by example" or "promoted by example," regardless of whether the official served in the capital or the provinces, these titles are only used if the official had taken office but had not yet received a title they were entitled to, or if they had been promoted and were entitled to a higher title. Those who never held office and only had a nominal rank are not given these notations.

Article 16:
For the grandfathers and fathers of wives and sons-in-law, their origins and last official positions are recorded. If they had no official position but were famous, only one generation is recorded. Occasionally, if they received high honors or positions, these are also recorded. For distant ancestors where information is scarce, the aim is to verify connections for marital relations, not to exaggerate. Titles and ranks are generally not recorded for sons-in-law either.

Article 17:
In the old genealogy, those without descendants were all marked as such. However, some have been missing in distant provinces for a long time, and it is unclear if they have descendants. Others in the village were kidnapped after the chaos and have not returned. It is inappropriate to label them as having no descendants, so this notation has been removed for consistency. If it was previously noted that they had an adopted heir but this cannot be verified, it is left as is, because having an unverified heir is different from having no heir at all.

Article 18:
In the old genealogy, birth and death dates were sometimes marked as "unknown" or "to be verified." In this revision, covering two hundred years, no verification has been made, so there is no need to wait any longer; "unknown" is written for all. However, if the old genealogy had partial information, it is retained. For newly added entries with unknown dates, the birth year is left blank, and the death year is marked with the character for "death."

Article 19:
For wives who remained chaste, regardless of whether they applied for imperial recognition, their deeds are already seen in the Inner Biographies, Biographies of Women, and the Worship section. The lineage table only notes "recognized for chastity" or "worshipped in the ancestral hall" without further elaboration. Applications for recognition are also verified. Only when the required years of chastity have not been met and there is no biography is a brief summary given for potential future application.

Article 20:
During the chaos of the Gengshen year (1860) of the Xianfeng reign, many in the clan died as martyrs. After verifying the facts, the words "died as martyr" are noted in the lineage table. Checking the county gazetteer, those already approved for worship in the Loyalty and Righteousness Ancestral Hall are marked with "posthumously honored" for men, and "recognized for chastity, worshipped in the Chaste and Heroic Women's Ancestral Hall" for women, with a brief account of their deeds in the Worship section. For men who were kidnapped, the words "was kidnapped" are temporarily written.

Article 21:
In the old genealogy, anyone with a biography or record was clearly noted in the lineage table, but those only in the short biographies were not. Now, anyone recorded in the short biographies or biographies of women is noted as "see such-and-such biography."

Article 22:
For marriages into families in other prefectures or counties, the specific county is noted. If in a different province, the province is noted.

Article 23:
For daughters who have not married but have been recognized for preserving their chastity, this is also noted in their father's entry in the lineage table.

Article 24:
In this revision, there are over ten branches that left long ago and whose whereabouts and survival are unknown; they are all marked as "left, unknown." We are anxious about this. It is now proposed that for those currently scattered across provinces whose locations can be determined, whether they are traveling for official business or living abroad, their current province or county should be noted to facilitate future clan reunification. In the old genealogy, the second and third branches of Lord Taibao's descendants living in Yunnan and Guangdong were clearly marked, and this is retained for the same reason. (End of Lineage Table section)

Article 25:
Compared to the men and women who died as martyrs in the Gengshen year, a separate "Worship" section is established, divided into the Loyalty and Righteousness Ancestral Hall and the Chaste and Heroic Women's Ancestral Hall. The years of posthumous honors are checked against the county gazetteer and recorded in detail. Those who volunteered are all marked. They are listed according to generational number to manifest the nation's grace and highlight loyalty and chastity. All those previously entered into the registers of famous officials, loyalty, and filial piety found scattered in the genealogy are collected and listed, with brief accounts of their deeds. The ancestral hall dedicated to Lord Taibao is placed first, and halls publicly built by the people under his command are also attached.

Article 26:
For chaste women who applied for imperial recognition, if the old genealogy did not detail the year, it is now clarified and fully recorded based on the county gazetteer. Additionally, those from the past who endured chastity but were not recognized, as well as recent chaste women, are sequentially investigated and added after the Chaste and Filial Women's Ancestral Hall section, to prepare for future applications for recognition.

Article 27:
Deeds recorded in the national history and prefecture/county gazetteers were only noted in the lineage table in the old genealogy. This time, they are all investigated and compiled into the first volume of biographies, with the front part as the upper volume.

Article 28:
Besides being arranged by generational number, the order of various categories also considers age. Those of the same age are ordered by month. For women, the husband's age is used as the basis. However, since the birth years of many martyrs are unknown, they are simply listed according to the generational number of the three major branches.

Article 29:
Academic degrees were not previously listed separately. Now, following the style of the gazetteer, from tribute student to advanced scholar, they are attached by examination year after the "Imperial Honors" record. This is to commemorate the nation's grace and reflect on ancestral virtue, not merely to glorify the local chronicles.

Article 30:
For the tombs of each branch, descendants who wish to draw diagrams may do so by donating funds separately, following the old rules. The same applies to newly added biographies and epitaphs.

Article 31:
The old records of ancestral hall and tomb lands lacked detailed figures, and many lands were lost after the chaos, making verification impossible. Now, everything is recorded item by item according to the joint ownership lists. Those without lists are placed in the lower volume.

Article 32:
All imperial honors and bestowed scrolls in the old genealogy were engraved in the "Imperial Honors" record. This time, for those whose edicts were issued but lost due to chaos and are difficult to recover, or for those who served outside and could not be contacted despite reminders, the year is briefly noted according to their official rank and attached at the end, to show that we dare not forget the nation's grace.

Article 33:
For anyone with commendable conduct to pass down, or those without descendants, or whose descendants have not yet compiled their stories, facts are gathered to write a biography. These are compiled in order along with biographies written by descendants of all branches and tomb inscriptions or family biographies written by others. Brief entries are listed in the short biographies.

Article 34:
For chaste women who served their parents and raised orphans for decades but have no dedicated biography, all facts are thoroughly investigated and briefly described according to the old genealogy's style, and included in the Biographies of Women. Other virtuous mothers, filial daughters-in-law, unmarried chaste women, and concubines with virtuous conduct are also recorded to ensure their reality is not obscured.

Article 35:
In the old genealogy, stray branches and adopted sons were appropriately placed in the supplementary genealogy. Now, with the clan growing daily, the stray branches are separated into their own volume. There are also branches where the lineage is unclear or clan relations are not harmonious; after careful consideration, these are attached at the end as a special category, strictly placing adopted sons in the supplementary genealogy.

Article 36:
For stray branches and the supplementary genealogy with noteworthy facts, they are also separately attached to the various categories to ensure they are not left out, and to show how deeply the ancestral virtue has influenced people and how widely it is felt.

Article 37:
All headings in the biographies follow the common public titles. This is an old rule, and it is followed this time.

Article 38:
The old genealogy had eighteen volumes. This time, the order generally follows the old rules, except that the original preface, new and old general rules, ancestral teachings, clan agreements, and table of contents are made the first volume. A volume for worship is added. The ancestral halls and tombs volume is split into two volumes. Additionally, each branch has added a volume of biographies. The epitaphs in the biographies are made into a separate volume. The stray branches in the supplementary genealogy are made the twenty-second volume, and the supplementary genealogy is compiled as the final volume. In total, there are twenty-four volumes.

Article 39:
The old genealogy occasionally had typographical errors (confusing similar-looking characters) and inconsistencies in birth/death dates between the tables and biographies. These have all been carefully checked and corrected one by one. However, since this time all categories were edited and proofread by one person without a dedicated proofreader, there is no guarantee that all old errors have been removed or that new errors have not been introduced. The style also has some inconsistencies. It is up to future gentlemen to pay attention, make corrections, and remedy any shortcomings.

Article 40:
This time, a total of sixty copies of the genealogy have been printed, numbered, and distributed. The distribution list is sent along with the books, all strictly following the old regulations.
