# 吕成 （春鸝公）

## 吕成 世表‌

* **吕成**
    - 行一字春鸝先世居宜兴父伯为公洪武时都指挥永乐三年谪戍陕西衞公泣请于所司愿代父往戍文皇悯之报可遂孑身往卒于戌所详载传中配杨氏携子迁居郡城为吾宗始祖葬郡城西门外怀南乡石塔新阡主穴丙山壬向子二长智次良

### 现代汉语 标点

吕成，在家族同辈中排行第一（行一），表字春鸝。他的祖先原本居住在宜兴县。他的父亲伯为公，在明太祖洪武年间担任“都指挥”（明代高级武官）。到了明成祖永乐三年，他父亲被贬谪发配到陕西的卫所去充军戍边。

吕成得知后，哭着向主管官员请求，愿意代替父亲前往戍边。明成祖（文皇）听闻后十分怜悯他，批准了他的请求。于是，吕成孤身一人前往陕西，最终死在了戍边的地方。他的详细事迹被专门记载在传记中。

他的妻子杨氏，带着儿子搬到了常州府城（郡城）居住，成为了我们这一支宗族的开基始祖。杨氏去世后，安葬在府城西门外怀南乡的石塔新主穴，墓地坐丙向壬（即坐南偏东、朝北偏西）。她生下了两个儿子，长子叫吕智，次子叫吕良。

### Lü Cheng on the Genealogy Table

Lü Cheng, the eldest son in his generation (ranked first), had the courtesy name Chunli. His ancestors originally lived in Yixing. His father, Lord Bowei, served as a Military Commander (Du Zhihui) during the Hongwu reign of the Ming Dynasty. In the third year of the Yongle era, his father was exiled and conscripted for military garrison duty at a garrison station (Wei) in Shaanxi.

Upon learning of this, Lü Cheng wept and pleaded with the local authorities, offering to take his father's place in exile. The Yongle Emperor was deeply moved by his filial piety and granted his request. Lü Cheng then traveled alone to the frontier and ultimately passed away at the garrison post. His detailed deeds are recorded in a dedicated biography.

His wife, Lady Yang, relocated with their son to the prefectural city, becoming the founding ancestor of our clan branch. She was buried at the Xinzhudian tomb site in Huainan Township, outside the west gate of the prefectural city. The grave is oriented with its back to the Bing (south-southeast) direction and facing the Ren (north-northwest) direction. She had two sons: the eldest was named Zhi, and the second was named Liang.

## 始迁祖春鸝公暨配杨太孺人合传

君姓吕氏，讳成，字春鹝。先世籍河南，宋南渡，十世祖讳好问，始居浙江婺州。七世祖讳祖泰，徙居江南宜兴县。考讳能，洪武中以都指挥使官京师，母储氏。

君为童子时，举止如成人。指挥公之官，综理家事，醇谨中有阔达器量，不屑屑乡曲所为。性纯孝，侍母夫人疾，不解衣者月余，夕三四起。

起购草药于肆中，不能得，冒霜雪，陟崎岖虎豹丛，一昼夜行数百里，得之，疾旋以愈，人咸以为孝感所致。后数年，母卒，哀毁骨立，为庐墓三年。服阕，随指挥公于京师，恂恂无所试。然一时奇杰之士，争以识君为快。

永乐三年，指挥公以事谪戍陕西，将行，君痛父年老，请于吏，愿以身代。吏以无故事不许，君扶伏于棘寺之间，哭累日不休，观者皆欷歔泣下，寺卿感动，奏之，得报可。乃辞指挥公以行，居久不能归，竟卒于戌所。子二，曰智，曰良。

夫人杨氏，同邑人。闻君行，矍然起曰：“夫能如是，吾奚憾哉！”自是绝荤腥，椎布操作，持家数十年，一身兼子职。翁没，丧葬尽礼，抚二子以成立，教诲严而有法，乡里至今称之不衰。以避宜兴徭，迁于郡城，没葬石塔塋，遂为武进吕氏始迁祖。

初，君以孑身远戍，家贫甚，夫人擕子零丁迁徙，若不能存一线者。然自后子孙绵延成巨族，多科第才德之士。

诗曰：孝子不匮，永锡尔类。又云：子子孙孙，勿替引之。惟孝故能刑于妻而大其后，余故详著之，明吕氏为一郡望由来有自，且示君之子孙，庶几无忘祖德云。

### 现代汉语 标点

君，姓吕，名成，字春鹝。祖先原籍河南，南宋南渡时，十世祖吕好问，开始在浙江婺州定居。七世祖吕祖泰，迁居江南宜兴县。父亲吕能，洪武年间以都指挥使的身份在京师做官，母亲储氏。

吕成还是孩童时，举止就像成人一样。指挥使父亲到京师赴任后，他管理家中事务，为人淳厚谨慎，有豁达的气量，不屑于做乡里那些琐屑之事。他天性纯孝，母亲生病时，他日夜侍奉，一个多月没脱衣服，每晚起来三四次。

有一次，他去街市上买草药，没买到，便冒着霜雪，攀登险峻且常有虎豹出没的山林，一昼夜行走了几百里才买到药，母亲的病很快就好了，人们都认为是他的孝心感动了上天。几年后，母亲去世，他悲痛得骨瘦如柴，为母亲守墓三年。守丧期满后，他随父亲到京师，恭敬谨慎，没有什么大的作为。但当时一些杰出的人士，都争着与他结识。

永乐三年，父亲因事被贬谪戍守陕西，即将启程，吕成心疼父亲年老，向官吏请求，愿自己代替父亲去戍边。官吏以没有这样的先例拒绝了他，他在官署前伏地哭泣，一连多日不停，围观的人都叹息流泪，官署长官被感动，上奏朝廷，得到了批准。于是他辞别父亲前往陕西，去了很久不能回来，最终死在了戍所。他有两个儿子，叫吕智、吕良。

夫人杨氏，是同乡人。听说吕成要代父戍边，她吃惊地站起来说：“丈夫能这样做，我还有什么遗憾呢！”从此她不再吃荤腥，穿着粗布衣服操持家务，支撑家庭几十年，一人承担起儿子的职责。公公去世后，她按礼制安葬，抚养两个儿子长大成人，教导他们严格而有方法，乡里至今称赞她。为躲避宜兴的徭役，她迁居到郡城，去世后葬在石塔塋，于是成为武进吕氏的始迁祖。

当初，吕成孤身一人远去戍边，家中十分贫穷，夫人带着儿子孤苦伶仃地迁徙，好像难以延续家族血脉。然而后来子孙繁衍成为大族，出了很多科举及第、有才有德的人。

诗说：孝子的孝心永不枯竭，永远赐予你福禄。又说：子子孙孙，不要废弃引之的孝道。只有孝才能感化妻子，使家族发展壮大，我所以详细记载这些，是为了表明吕氏成为一郡望族是有原因的，并且告诫吕成的子孙，希望他们不要忘记祖先的德行。

### Joint Biography of Lv Cheng and His Wife Lady Yang

Mr. Lv Cheng, surnamed Lv with the given name Cheng and the style name Chunyi, traced his ancestry back to Henan. During the Southern Song Dynasty's southern migration, his tenth-generation ancestor, Lv Haowen, initially settled in Wuzhou, Zhejiang. His seventh-generation ancestor, Lv Zutai, relocated to Yixing County in the Jiangnan region. His father, Lv Neng, served as a commander-in-chief in the capital during the Hongwu reign period of the Ming Dynasty, and his mother was surnamed Chu.

Even as a child, Lv Cheng conducted himself with the demeanor of an adult. After his father assumed office in the capital, Lv Cheng managed household affairs with purity, kindness, and prudence, possessing a magnanimous disposition and disdaining petty matters within the village. Naturally filial, when his mother fell ill, he attended to her day and night, remaining clothed for over a month and rising three to four times each night.

Once, when purchasing herbal medicine in the marketplace proved futile, he braved frost and snow, ascending perilous, tiger- and leopard-infested mountains and forests. Traveling hundreds of miles in a day and night, he finally acquired the medicine, leading to his mother's swift recovery. People attributed this to his filial piety moving the heavens. Several years later, when his mother passed away, he grieved until emaciated and kept vigil at her grave for three years. After the mourning period ended, he followed his father to the capital, remaining respectful and cautious without significant accomplishments. Nevertheless, prominent figures of the era contended to acquaint themselves with him.

In the third year of the Yongle reign period, his father faced demotion to guard duty in Shaanxi. Seeing his elderly father preparing to depart, Lv Cheng requested to take his father's place. Officials rejected him, citing no precedent, but he wept prostrate before the yamen (government office) for days, drawing crowds who sighed and wept. Moved, the official petitioned the court and gained approval. Thus, Lv Cheng bade his father farewell and departed for Shaanxi, where he remained for an extended period, ultimately dying at his post. He had two sons, named Lv Zhi and Lv Liang.

Lady Yang, his wife, hailed from the same hometown. Upon hearing of Lv Cheng's intention to take his father's place on guard duty, she rose in astonishment, declaring, "If my husband can act thus, what regret have I?" From that moment, she abstained from meat, wore coarse clothing, managed household affairs, and sustained the family for decades, assuming her son's responsibilities alone. After her father-in-law's passing, she arranged his burial according to ritual, raised and educated her two sons with strictness and method, earning the village's praise to this day. To evade Yixing's corvée labor, she relocated to the prefectural city and, after her death, was interred at Shita Cemetery, becoming the progenitor of the Wujin Lv lineage.

Initially, Lv Cheng's solitary departure for guard duty left the family destitute. Lady Yang, leading their sons in desolate migration, seemed unlikely to perpetuate the lineage. However, their descendants flourished into a prominent clan, producing numerous scholarly and virtuous talents who excelled in imperial examinations.

As the poem goes: "A filial son's devotion never dries up, eternally bestowing blessings upon you." It also states: "Descendants, do not abandon Yin Zhi's filial piety." Only through filial piety can one inspire one's wife and expand the family. I detail these events to demonstrate why the Lv family became a respected lineage within the prefecture and to admonish Lv Cheng's descendants, urging them not to forget their ancestors' virtues.

### 现代汉语 标点

我恭敬地查阅了旧版家谱，发现其中有如下记载：泰然公的长子名叫“凤载”，表字“朝阳”；忠言公名叫“载隐”，记载说他隐居在宜兴梁华滨；云卿公名下只记载了一个儿子叫“光弼”；伯为公的次子盛公，记载他居住在荆溪“涧滨”。此外，老谱在记载四子“戊”公时，仅仅只写了他表字“荆甫”，是个县学生员（秀才）。

如今，我经过核对《宜兴宗谱》，发现情况并非如此：
凤公的表字其实是“东昇”；忠言公是前往渭滨务农，于是便在那里安了家，老谱中还记载了他葬在石码头山；云卿公其实生了两个儿子，长子叫光弼，表字荆泉，次子叫光鼎，表字茂泉，后来迁居到了金陵（南京）的龙江关（只是光鼎公当年没有被列入世系表中）；伯为公的次子盛公，是世世代代居住在荆溪“渭滨”的，绝对不是“涧滨”。

经过考证，“涧滨”距离“渭滨”有十里地，那里虽然也有姓吕的人居住，但他们是后来从别处迁过去的，和我们并不是同一个宗族。至于四子“戊”公的后代，有居住在河北、河南的分支，其实都是戊公的后裔，后来也与渭滨的主谱合并了。

庚子年我们常州府重修家谱时，大概是因为传闻有误、说法不一，所以旧谱中有很多不符合事实的地方。今天，我依据《宜兴宗谱》的记载，对这些问题进行了全面的校对和修正，希望让后世子孙在查阅家谱时，能够看到准确的历史。

光绪戊寅年（1878年）深秋，继午恭敬地记述

---

**核心信息提炼：**
1. **名字与字号纠错**：凤载（应为凤，字东昇）、载隐（应为忠言，去渭滨务农）。
2. **子嗣遗漏补充**：云卿公其实有两个儿子，老谱漏掉了次子光鼎（迁居南京）。
3. **地名“打假”**：老谱写的“涧滨”是错的，正确应该是“渭滨”。修谱人还特意强调，涧滨那边虽然有吕姓，但跟我们不是亲戚（“并非一族”）。
4. **修谱态度**：体现了古代修谱人“实事求是、严谨考证”的治学态度，不盲从旧谱，敢于纠正前人错误。

### The Corrections to the The Connection Years 

I have carefully examined the old genealogy, which recorded the following: Tai Ran Gong's eldest son was named Fengzai, with the courtesy name Chaoyang; Zhongyan Gong was named Zaiyin, and the record states that he lived in seclusion at Lianghuabin in Yixing; Yunqing Gong had only one recorded son, Guangbi; and Bo Wei Gong's second son, Sheng Gong, was said to reside at Jianbin in Jingxi. Furthermore, regarding the fourth son, Wu Gong, the old record merely noted his courtesy name, Jingfu, and his status as a county student (Xiucai).

Today, after cross-referencing with the *Yixing Clan Genealogy*, I have found the following corrections: 
Gong Feng's courtesy name was actually Dongsheng. Zhongyan Gong moved to Weibin to farm and settled there; the records also note that he was buried at Shimatou Mountain. Yunqing Gong actually had two sons: the eldest, Guangbi, courtesy name Jingquan, and the second, Guangding, courtesy name Maoquan, who later relocated to Longjiang Pass in Jinling (Nanjing). (Guangding was simply omitted from the old lineage chart.) Bo Wei Gong's second son, Sheng Gong, lived in Weibin, Jingxi for generations, not Jianbin.

Upon verification, Jianbin is ten li away from Weibin. Although people with the same surname reside there, they migrated from elsewhere and do not belong to our clan. As for the descendants of the fourth son, Wu Gong, there are branches residing in Hebei and Henan; they are all his progeny, and their lineage was later merged into the main Weibin genealogy.

During the Gengzi year when our Changzhou genealogy was compiled, discrepancies arose due to inaccurate oral transmission and conflicting accounts, resulting in many errors in the old records. Today, I have thoroughly revised and corrected these matters based on the *Yixing Clan Genealogy*, so that future generations may have an accurate history to consult.

Respectfully recorded by Jiwu in the autumn of the Wuyin year of the Guangxu era (1878).

---

**Key Takeaways (The Compiler's Fact-Checking Notes):**
1. **Name & Courtesy Name Corrections**: Fengzai (should be Feng, courtesy name Dongsheng); Zaiyin (should be Zhongyan, who went to Weibin to farm).
2. **Missing Descendants Added**: Yunqing Gong actually had two sons; the old genealogy omitted the second son, Guangding (who moved to Nanjing).
3. **Geographical "Debunking"**: The old record's "Jianbin" was incorrect; it should be "Weibin". The compiler specifically emphasized that although there are Lü-surnamed people in Jianbin, they are not related to this branch ("do not belong to our clan").
4. **Genealogical Rigor**: This reflects the ancient genealogist's scholarly attitude of "seeking truth from facts." The compiler did not blindly follow the old records but dared to correct historical errors based on cross-referencing.
