# 毘陵呂氏增修族譜 序

---

### 毘陵呂氏增修族譜序 (1905)

續修宗譜序

民之生也必有攸繫，國以義出治，家以親相及。古之聖王以孝治天下，臣子之有功德者，賞必延於其子孫。是以胙之土而命之氏，使有以自別於氓庶，所以訓明德、勸有功也。《易》曰：「積善之家必有餘慶，積不善之家必有餘殃。」

祖宗之慶善及於子孫，子孫苟謹守焉，而罔或隕越，則亦足以保其家矣。吾族自明永樂間遷常州，順治癸巳始有扶譜之作，乾隆辛亥始修，道光庚子再修。洪楊亂後，迄光緒丙子而第三次修輯之譜始成，自始事迄告竣凡八年。以前之闕而不修者既久，且中更喪亂也。歲戊戌，扶松、繼午自粵歸，與金誠等始創修輯之議。時則吾族之散處城鄉者日益繁衍，又有寄居他省者，恐其事久而難成也。金誠、繼午乃首任其經費十之五，而與修譜者什一，以籌費謂之丁錢，及是免焉。

既合族人之在常者而公議之，又馳書遠道告以修輯之舉。金誠、繼午與十八世續熙、十九世學恩等力任校讎纂輯之勞。費有不足，全誠又以獨力任之。歲乙巳十二月事成，都示偉孫使為之序。伏念呂氏先世出自四嶽，著績虞夏，歷漢唐宋，代有聞人。子孫之散處大江南北者，世為望族，迄於今不衰，蓋祖宗之遺澤深矣。後嗣之子孫雖不敢仰望祖宗之明德，然其處鄉里有服疇力穡、居城邑者懋遷有無、列庠序者為世所式矜、登朝雲者與國同休戚，是皆祖宗之遺澤有以庇蔭其子孫也。聖人不云乎：「事親孝則忠可移於君，順可移於長。」百行之元良以孝為本，宗為子孫者苟能遠念其祖宗之勳猷，兢兢焉惟弗克負荷是懼，其庶幾可以免於危乎？然後知聖人敬宗收族、以孝治天下之微意也。乃謹述此意以為之序。

光緒三十一年乙巳冬，十五世孫偉孫謹序。

### 现代汉语 标点

续修宗谱序

人活在世上，必然有维系其生存的根本。国家靠“义”来治理，家庭靠“亲情”来维系。古代的圣明君王用孝道来治理天下，臣子中有功德的，赏赐一定会延及到他们的子孙。因此分封土地、赐予姓氏，让他们有别于普通百姓，以此来彰显美德、鼓励有功之人。《易经》说：“积善的家庭必然有多余的福庆，积不善的家庭必然有多余的灾祸。”

祖宗积累的福泽惠及子孙，子孙如果能谨慎地守住这份家业，不使其衰败陨落，也就足以保全家族了。我们家族从明朝永乐年间迁徙到常州，顺治癸巳年（1653年）开始有了修谱的举动，乾隆辛亥年（1791年）才开始正式修撰，道光庚子年（1840年）进行了第二次修撰。在太平天国战乱（洪杨之乱）之后，直到光绪丙子年（1876年）第三次修辑的族谱才完成，从开始办事到最终完成总共花了八年时间。在此之前，因为长久没有修谱，中间又经历了战乱丧事，导致很多内容缺失。

光绪戊戌年（1898年），扶松、继午从广东回来，和金诚等人开始倡议重新修辑族谱。当时我们家族散居在城乡各地的人日益增多，还有寄居在其他省份的族人，大家担心这件事拖久了就难以做成。金诚、继午于是首先承担了十分之五的经费，而参与修谱的人承担十分之一，通过筹集费用来作为丁钱（人头税或修谱费），到了这次修谱时这笔费用就免除了。

既然召集了在常州的族人公开商议，又写信给远方的族人告知修谱这件事。金诚、继午与十八世孙续熙、十九世孙学恩等人全力承担了校对和编纂的辛劳工作。如果经费有不足的地方，金诚又独自承担了费用。乙巳年（1905年）十二月事情办成，大家都示意让伟孙来写这篇序言。

我想吕氏的先世出自四岳，在虞夏时期就有功绩，历经汉、唐、宋各代，都有有名望的人。子孙散居在大江南北的，世代都是望族，直到今天也没有衰落，这大概都是祖宗遗留下来的恩泽深厚啊。后世的子孙虽然不敢仰望祖宗的高尚品德，但是那些居住在乡里努力耕作的，居住在城镇经商互通有无的，在学校读书被世人作为榜样的，以及在朝廷做官与国家同甘共苦的，这些都是祖宗的遗泽在庇荫着子孙啊。圣人不是说吗：“侍奉父母孝顺，那么这种忠心就可以移用到君主身上；顺从兄长，那么这种顺从就可以移用到长辈身上。”百种德行的根本在于孝，作为宗族的子孙，如果能长远地感念祖宗的功绩，兢兢业业地唯恐不能承担重任，那大概就可以免于危亡了吧？这样之后就知道圣人敬重宗族、凝聚族人，用孝道治理天下的深意了。于是我谨陈述这番心意作为序言。

光绪三十一年（1905年）乙巳年冬天，十五世孙伟孙恭敬作序。

### Preface to the Continued Revision of the Clan Genealogy

The life of the people must have something to bind it. The state is governed by righteousness, and the family is bound by kinship. The sage kings of old governed the world with filial piety. For ministers and subjects who had merit and virtue, rewards were surely extended to their descendants. Therefore, land was enfeoffed and surnames were bestowed, giving them a distinction from the common people. This was done to illustrate virtuous conduct and encourage meritorious service. The Book of Changes says: "A family that accumulates good deeds will surely have surplus blessings; a family that accumulates evil deeds will surely have surplus misfortunes."

The accumulated goodness of our ancestors has benefited their descendants. If the descendants carefully preserve it and do not let it decline, it is enough to protect the family. Our clan moved to Changzhou during the Yongle period of the Ming Dynasty. The first genealogy was initiated in the Guisi year of the Shunzhi reign (1653). It was first revised in the Xinhai year of the Qianlong reign (1791) and revised again in the Gengzi year of the Daoguang reign (1840). After the chaos of the Hong-Yang Rebellion (Taiping Heavenly Kingdom), the third revision of the genealogy was finally completed in the Bingzi year of the Guangxu reign (1876). From the beginning of the work to its completion, it took eight years. Before this, the genealogy had not been revised for a long time, and it had also experienced war and turmoil in the interim.

In the Wuxu year (1898), Fusong and Jiwu returned from Guangdong and, together with Jincheng and others, initiated the proposal to revise the genealogy. At that time, our clan members scattered in the cities and countryside were multiplying daily, and there were also those living in other provinces. We feared that if the matter dragged on, it would be difficult to accomplish. Jincheng and Jiwu therefore took the lead in bearing five-tenths of the expenses, while those participating in the revision contributed one-tenth each, which was called the "ding qian" (head tax). With this, the funds were secured.

After gathering the clan members in Changzhou for public discussion, letters were also sent to distant places to inform them of the revision project. Jincheng, Jiwu, together with the 18th-generation descendant Xu Xi and the 19th-generation descendant Xue En, vigorously undertook the arduous work of proofreading and compiling. When the expenses were insufficient, Jincheng bore the burden alone. In the twelfth month of the Yisi year (1905), the work was completed. Everyone indicated that Wei Sun should write the preface.
Reflecting on our Lü clan's ancestors, they originated from the Four Peaks (Si Yue) and achieved merits in the Yu and Xia dynasties. Throughout the Han, Tang, and Song dynasties, there were famous figures in every generation. The descendants scattered north and south of the Yangtze River have been a prominent clan for generations and have not declined to this day. This is surely because the legacy left by our ancestors is profound. Although the descendants of later generations dare not look up to the bright virtue of our ancestors, whether they are in the countryside tilling the fields, in the cities engaged in trade, in schools serving as role models for the world, or in the imperial court sharing the fortunes of the nation, all of this is due to the protection and blessing of our ancestors' legacy. Did not the sage say: "If one is filial to parents, loyalty can be transferred to the ruler; if one is obedient at home, it can be transferred to elders." Filial piety is the foundation of all virtues. If the descendants of the clan can think deeply and from afar of the achievements of their ancestors, and with reverence fear that they cannot bear the responsibility, then perhaps they can avoid danger. Only then can one understand the profound intention of the sages in honoring the clan, gathering the family, and governing the world with filial piety. Therefore, I respectfully set forth this meaning as a preface.

Respectfully prefaced by Wei Sun, the 15th-generation descendant, in the winter of the Yisi year, the 31st year of the Guangxu reign (1905).
