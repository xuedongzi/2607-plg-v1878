### 现代汉语 简体字

自从古代宗法制度废除后，谱牒便兴起了。谱牒的创作，是为了尊崇祖先、敬重宗族、凝聚族人，其中就蕴含着大宗和小宗的意义。《周官》中所说的奠定世系，就是族谱的起源。唐代重视门第，对谱学的研究更加深入；宋代的欧阳氏和苏氏又倡导建立图谱，阐明体例义理，于是成为后世修谱的典范。

我们吕氏是四岳的后裔，自周秦以来，代有名人。其中有世系、仕宦、生卒可记载的，就从唐代的嵩山公开始。从唐到宋，家族更加庞大，期间名儒贤臣、忠良之士，在政事文章方面，前后辉映，因此美名远扬，足以庇佑子孙。

始迁祖春鸝公，在明洪武年间代替父亲戍守陕西卫，始迁祖母杨夫人从宜兴迁至武进，成为武进吕氏的始祖。第五世祖有铉、镛、镇，是三大分祖。传至第九世，沅的曾祖太保公，在朝廷殿试中对策第一，官至太子太保宏文院大学士。自春鸝公以孝行起家，到太保公时吕氏开始显赫。太保公将孝道转化为忠心，忠厚纯朴，子孙不应忘记先人的德行，更要继承发扬。孝道必然要尊崇祖先，尊崇祖先必然要敬重宗族，敬重宗族必然要凝聚族人。如此一来，谱牒的创作岂不是非常重要吗？苏明允说：“起初一个人的身体，分散开来就像街上的行人。”我们吕氏族人散居在各地，日益繁衍，以至于询问其支派，却茫然不知如何作答，几乎不像同宗之人。以祖宗的视角来看，都是子孙；而这些子孙却彼此陌生，几乎如同路人。我们武进吕氏距离首次修谱已经有一百多年了。丁丑年，我前往山右，仲兄当时任万泉令，在处理政事的闲暇，常常担忧族谱模糊不清，说：“这是先父未完成的心愿，是我和你的责任。”丁亥年，我从太原归来，兄长又提及此事。我回来后便向从兄朝柱请示，说：“此事不可拖延，我早已有此志向。”不久，两位兄长先后离世。族中能够承担此事的人，如尔昌、尔益都在任上去世，荣光又远在将乐为官，多年蹉跎，而我也渐渐老去，几乎担心此事无法完成。如今有幸看到族谱修成，在家塾设立公所，召集共同修谱之人，勤勤恳恳、坚定不移、立志完成修谱大业的，莫过于尔喆；尔禧任桐乡令，往来书信，加以考察，寒暑无间，心力交瘁，统筹此事以期望早日完成的，莫过于我；梓和宪榊作为辅助，搜集传记，核实名实，岳主又加以复核，参与斟酌详审、始终操持此事的，有如篆勤奋做事、校正编次的，有如椿倚综合核查各分世次生卒的，汇合各种事务，费凤翽也出了大力。刻版印刷的费用，是汇集三分之力而成的。

我们族人分散难以稽查，族谱又长久未修，岁月流逝，各房子孙有的客居远方，有的外出为官，有的务农读书，有的经商从事技艺，能够承担修谱之事的人能够聚集在一个庭院，更是难上加难。因此修谱之事时作时辍，开始修谱的原因与完成修谱的日子，前后相隔二十多年。如今一旦完成，其中的辛苦可想而知。

远祖始迁祖以下，分立表系，彰显了大宗小宗的遗意，明确了支派，分清了世次，奠定了疏戚尊卑的关系，记载了国恩，详述了祠墓，清楚了居徙。文字简洁质朴，不符合道义的删除，不真实的刊正，未彰显其品行的补充，官爵、年龄、婚姻、子女，无不详细完备。

从今以后，要知道修谱的艰难，知道长久不修的遗憾。各分之人，每年记录其亲属的名字履历、男女嫁娶生卒的月日，十年一汇总，三十年刻版颁发。这就是所说的尊祖敬宗收族，是我深切的期望。如果说此谱的修成我有功劳，那我却耽误至今，正要自责不已呢。

十一世孙沅谨序

### Preface to the Revised Genealogy of the Lü Clan in Piling (1785)

Since ancient times, the patriarchal clan system has been abolished, and genealogical records have flourished. The creation of genealogical records aims to honor ancestors, respect the clan, and unite family members, embodying the concepts of the major and minor lineages. What is referred to as establishing the lineage system in "The Rites of Zhou" is the origin of genealogical records. In the Tang Dynasty, social status was highly valued, and the study of genealogy was further explored. In the Song Dynasty, the Ouyang and Su families advocated the establishment of genealogical diagrams and explanations, clarifying the principles and meanings, thus becoming the model for genealogical research in later generations.

We, the Lü family, are descendants of the Four Peaks. Since the Qin and Zhou dynasties, there have been notable figures in each generation. Those with recordable lineage, official positions, and dates of birth and death began with Master Songshan in the Tang Dynasty. From the Tang to the Song Dynasty, the clan grew larger, with famous scholars, capable ministers, virtuous and loyal officials, and outstanding political and literary figures succeeding each other in beauty, thus maintaining a good reputation and providing for their descendants.

The founding ancestor, Chunli, in the Hongwu reign of the Ming Dynasty, took his father's place to garrison the Shaanxi Guard. He moved with his grandmother, Madam Yang, from Yixing to Wujin, becoming the founding ancestor of the Wujin Lü family. The fifth-generation ancestors, Xuan, Yong, and Zhen, became the three major branch ancestors. By the ninth generation, my great-grandfather, the Grand Tutor, ranked first in the palace examination and served as the Grand Secretary of the Hongwen Academy under the Crown Prince. Starting from Chunli's rise through filial piety, the Lü family became prominent with the Grand Tutor. The Grand Tutor transformed filial piety into loyalty, being sincere and steadfast. Descendants should not forget the virtues of their ancestors and should inherit and carry them forward.

Filial piety necessitates respecting ancestors, respecting ancestors necessitates revering the clan, and revering the clan necessitates uniting family members. If so, how important is the creation of genealogical records! Su Mingyun said, "At first, one person's body is divided, and then it becomes like people on the street." Our Lü family members are scattered throughout the villages, multiplying day by day, to the point where some, when asked about their branches, are at a loss for words, almost like strangers. From the perspective of ancestors, they are all descendants; yet these descendants are unfamiliar with each other, almost like strangers on the street. Our Wujin Lü family has been more than a hundred years since the first compilation of the genealogy. In the year of Dingchou, I went to Shanyou, where my second elder brother was serving as the magistrate of Wanquan. In his spare time from official duties, he often worried about the blurred genealogy, saying, "This is the unfinished wish of our late father, and our responsibility." In the year of Dinghai, when I was about to return from Taiyuan, my brother mentioned it again. Upon my return, I asked my cousin Chaozhu, saying, "This cannot be delayed. I have had this intention for a long time." Soon after, both brothers passed away. Among the clan members who could undertake this task, such as Erchang and Eryi, died while in office, and Rongguang was far away serving as an official in Jiangle. After years of setbacks, and I myself growing old, I almost feared that this task could not be accomplished.

Fortunately, we have now witnessed its completion! A public office was set up in the family school to gather those working together. Those who are diligent and determined to complete the task are like Erzhe. When Erxiao served as the magistrate of Tongxiang, he exchanged letters back and forth, adding investigations, working tirelessly through cold and heat, and exerting both mental and physical effort. Those who oversaw the matter in order to expedite its completion are like me, with Zixian and Nan as assistants, collecting prefaces and biographies, verifying names and facts, and Yuezhuzhi and Guangfu conducting further reviews, considering and deliberating carefully throughout the entire process. Like Zhuan's diligence in handling affairs and correcting and compiling, and like Chunyijiao's comprehensive verification of the birth and death dates of the two major branches, with Fei Fenghui making significant contributions. The funds for engraving were pooled from the three branches.

Our clan is scattered and difficult to trace, and the genealogy has been neglected for a long time. The various descendants are either far away, serving as officials, farming or studying, or engaged in business and crafts. It is especially difficult to gather those capable of working on the genealogy in one place at the right time. Therefore, the work was started and stopped repeatedly, spanning over twenty years from the beginning to the end. Now that it is completed in one day, the hardships involved are immense.

Below the founding ancestor, separate lineage charts are established, highlighting the intentions of the major and minor lineages, clarifying the branches and generations, establishing the relationships between relatives and the hierarchy of respect and humility, recording the imperial favors, detailing the ancestral temples and graves, and clarifying the residences and relocations. The text is concise and simple, deleting what does not conform to righteousness, amending what does not convey the truth, and supplementing what does not彰显 (should be "manifest" or "show", but here it might be a typo, so I'll interpret as "make known") the virtues. Official ranks, ages, marriages, and children are all detailed.

From now on, we know the difficulty of compiling the genealogy and the regret of not revising it for a long time. Each branch should record the names and resumes of their relatives, as well as the marriage and birth and death dates of men and women, every year, and gather every ten years, and engrave and distribute every thirty years. This is what is meant by respecting ancestors, revering the clan, and uniting family members, which I deeply hope for. If it is said that I have contributed to the completion of this genealogy, but I have delayed it until now, and I am just about to blame myself.

The eleventh-generation descendant, Yuan, respectfully writes this preface.
