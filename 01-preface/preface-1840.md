# 毘陵呂氏族譜 原序

---

### 道光二十年歲次庚子重修族譜序（1840）

```text
家之有譜所以奠繫世合族屬定親疎辨長幼者也葉茂
枝繁發於一本川分瀆演祗此原泉此譜之修所以不容
已乎吾呂氏遷居毘陵已五百年前明未有譜牒順治癸
巳始輯成書乾隆辛亥重加修纂而體例已備然其時距
始遷四百餘載即距創譜之日亦百數十年散處鄉邑支
衍派分闕軼者殆居其半今又五十年矣若不早修輯將
更有莫可尋溯者數年前子珊官豫子班官浙志恒官閩
僉商舉修以採葺乏人遷延未果志恒旋殉王事子班亦
謝世蹉跎數年今子珊解組歸適侄孫兄弟以憂居里欲
成先人志集眾會議詢謀僉同乃於己亥夏仲開纂遵舊
章而損益之閱一載竣事統其事以董厥成者子珊也倡
議興修志在必行者侄孫也職司纂訂終始其事者佶孫
也綜理搜訪則有倫敘從而參酌者子環子琳也外分校
訂正及各分督捐則合羣力而為之者承慶行齒居長蓄
志已久今幸譜事有成初心竊慰尤願後之人循三十年
一修之約按時編葺勿使久而難稽抑更有冀者吾呂氏
顯於唐盛於宋名臣相業彪炳史策洎吾始遷祖春鸝公
以孝行聞世德相承簪纓不絕毘陵數族望者咸推呂氏
積厚者流光抑亦培養之有其道也今吾族類多鄉黨自
好誠能戶誦讀家弦歌胥明於體立用行之學以克保中
原文獻之傳是亦祖宗所厚望者夫
十二世孫承慶謹序
```

### 现代汉语 简体字

**道光二十年岁次庚子重修族谱序（1840）**

家族编修族谱，是为了确定世系传承、团结族人、厘清亲疏关系、辨别长幼次序。就像树木枝叶茂盛都源于同一个根本，河流分支流淌都来自同一个源头。因此族谱的修撰，是刻不容缓的事情啊！

我们吕氏家族迁居常州（毗陵），已经有五百年了。明朝以前没有族谱，顺治癸巳年（1653年）才首次编纂成书；乾隆辛亥年（1791年）再次修纂，体例已经完备。然而那时距离始迁祖迁来已有四百多年，即使距离首次创谱也有一百几十年。族人散居在各地乡村城镇，支派繁衍分化，缺失遗漏的内容几乎占了一半。

现在又过了五十年，如果不尽早修辑，将来会有更多无法追溯考证的内容。数年前，子珊在河南做官，子班在浙江做官，志恒在福建做官，我们共同商议重修族谱。但因缺乏采集整理资料的人手，一再拖延未能完成。后来志恒因公殉职，子班也去世了。蹉跎了数年，如今子珊卸任回乡，恰逢侄孙兄弟因服丧在家，想要完成先人的遗愿，于是召集众人开会商议，大家意见一致。于是在己亥年（1839年）仲夏开始编纂，遵循旧章程并加以增减调整，经过一年完成此事。

统筹全局、监督完成的是子珊；倡议兴修、志在必行的是侄孙；负责纂写订正、自始至终操办的是佶孙；综合整理、搜集访查的，有伦叙；从而参与斟酌讨论的，有子环、子琳；此外分校订正以及各分支督促捐款，则是集合众人之力共同完成的，其中承庆我年龄最长、辈分最高，这个心愿蓄积已久，如今幸而族谱修成，内心略感欣慰。

我尤其希望后代子孙，遵循三十年一修的约定，按时编修辑录，不要让时间拖得太久而难以查考。

我还有更深的期望：我们吕氏家族在唐代显赫、在宋代兴盛，名臣辈出、宰相功业，在史册中光辉灿烂。自我们始迁祖春公以孝行闻名于世，世代德行相承，官宦不绝。常州地区几个望族，都推崇吕氏。积德深厚者福泽绵长，这也是因为培养后代有其正道啊！

如今我们族人多在乡里自我修养，如果能够家家户户诵读诗书、家家弦歌不辍，都明白修身立本、经世致用的学问，从而保持中原文献的传承，这也是祖宗对我们的深切期望啊！

十二世孙 承庆 谨序

---

**注释：**
- **毗陵**：今江苏常州一带
- **簪缨**：古代官员的冠饰，借指做官
- **解组**：卸去官职
- **以忧居里**：因父母去世在家服丧
- **询谋同**：大家商议意见一致

### Preface to the Revised Genealogy of the Lü Clan (1840)

**20th Year of Daoguang Reign, Gengzi Year (1840)**

The compilation of a family genealogy serves to establish lineage succession, unite clan members, clarify degrees of kinship, and distinguish seniority. Just as flourishing branches and leaves all spring from a single root, and as tributaries all flow from the same source, the compilation of our genealogy is a matter that brooks no delay!

Our Lü clan has resided in Piling (modern-day Changzhou) for five hundred years. Before the Ming Dynasty, there was no genealogical record. The first compilation was completed in the Guisi year of Shunzhi (1653); it was reconstructed in the Xinhai year of Qianlong (1791), at which time the format and rules were already well-established. However, at that time, it had been over four hundred years since our founding ancestor migrated here, and even over a century since the first genealogy was created. Our clan members were scattered across various towns and villages; branches had multiplied and diverged, and nearly half of the records were incomplete or lost.

Now another fifty years have passed. If we do not compile the genealogy soon, there will be even more that cannot be traced or verified. Several years ago, Zishan was serving as an official in Henan, Ziban in Zhejiang, and Zhiheng in Fujian. We discussed reconstructing the genealogy together, but due to lack of personnel to collect and organize materials, it was repeatedly postponed and never came to fruition. Later, Zhiheng died in the line of duty, and Ziban also passed away. After years of delay, Zishan has now retired and returned home. Coincidentally, my grandnephews were at home mourning the death of their parents, and wishing to fulfill the aspirations of our ancestors, we gathered everyone for a meeting. With unanimous agreement, we began the compilation in the mid-summer of the Jihai year (1839), following the old format while making appropriate additions and deletions. The work was completed after one year.

Those who oversaw the entire project and supervised its completion: Zishan.
Those who proposed the reconstruction and were determined to see it through: my grandnephews.
Those who were responsible for compilation and correction, seeing the project through from beginning to end: Jisun.
Those who coordinated collection and investigation: Lunxu.
Those who participated in discussions and consultations: Zihuan and Zilin.
As for the distribution of proofreading duties and the supervision of donations from various branches, this was accomplished through the collective efforts of all. Among them, I, Chengqing, being the eldest in age and seniority, had long held this aspiration. Now that the genealogy has been successfully completed, my heart is somewhat comforted.

I especially hope that future generations will follow the agreement of revising the genealogy every thirty years, compiling it on schedule, and not allowing so much time to pass that it becomes difficult to verify.

I have an even deeper hope: Our Lü clan was prominent during the Tang Dynasty and flourished during the Song Dynasty. Our distinguished ministers and chancellors' achievements shine brilliantly in the historical records. Since our founding ancestor, Chunli Gong, became known for his filial piety, virtuous conduct has been passed down through generations, and official positions have continued uninterrupted. Among the several prestigious clans in Changzhou, all acknowledge the Lü clan. Those who accumulate virtue deeply enjoy long-lasting glory—this is also because there is a proper way to cultivate future generations!

Nowadays, many of our clan members cultivate themselves within their local communities. If we can ensure that every household recites the classics and every family engages in scholarly pursuits, all understanding the learning of establishing moral character and applying it to governance, thereby preserving the transmission of Central Plains literature and culture, this too is the profound hope our ancestors held for us!

**12th Generation Descendant, Chengqing, respectfully composed this preface**

---

**Notes:**
- **Piling (毗陵)**: Ancient name for the Changzhou area in Jiangsu Province
- **Zan Ying (簪缨)**: Hairpins and cap ribbons worn by officials; metaphor for holding government office
- **Jie Zu (解组)**: To resign from official position (literally "to untie the official seal")
- **Yi You Ju Li (以忧居里)**: To be at home observing mourning for deceased parents
- **Xun Mou Qian Tong (询谋同)**: After consultation, everyone is in agreement
